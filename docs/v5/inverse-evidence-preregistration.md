# V5-RBG-4 preregistration: inverse evidence in strategic choice

**Status:** frozen after RBG-3 and before RBG-4 dataset materialization or model
execution.

RBG-2 produced a large assertion/action dissociation with four redundant policy
demonstrations and a prose mapping. RBG-3 used a history-free consequence table
and produced only seven residual errors. Cross-study comparison cannot identify
which prompt difference caused the attenuation. RBG-4 crosses both factors within
fresh structurally matched games.

## Factorial

Twenty-four fresh bases cross strategic receiver/device framing, aligned/opposed
goals, assertions/opaque tokens, absent/four-example redundant history, and
prose/table explicit mapping. The response policy is always contrarian. Every row
receives a direct action query and independent consequence reports for both options.

## Frozen gates

Opaque action accuracy and all option-report accuracy must each be at least 95%.
The inverse-evidence hypothesis requires that redundant history reduce opposed
assertion action accuracy by at least 20 points relative to no history, while the
absolute report-accuracy gap is at most five points, with exact base-game p<.05.

Semantic specificity requires the history harm for assertions to exceed the history
harm for opaque tokens by at least 20 points with exact base-game p<.05. The exact
RBG-2-like cell—strategic, opposed, assertion, redundant history, prose mapping—must
be at most 50% action accurate while reports and matched opaque actions remain at
least 90%.

Failure of the within-study history contrast archives the RBG-2/RBG-3 difference
as prompt-family instability. No internal or mechanistic claim is licensed.
