# V5-RBG-4 — inverse-evidence replication

## The question

Can adding more correct evidence make a strategic action worse while leaving a
separate consequence report intact?

This study held the game fixed and compared no history with four correct,
redundant demonstrations. It also compared a prose rule with the same rule in a
compact consequence table, and meaningful assertions with opaque tokens.

## What happened

All frozen gates passed. In opposed contrarian games, meaningful assertion
accuracy fell from 79.17% without history to 55.21% with the four demonstrations.
Consequence reports stayed at 98.96%, and opaque actions stayed at 100%.

The harm was concentrated in prose: 75.00% became 27.08%. The table condition
stayed at 83.33% with or without history. This makes the result more specific
than “longer context is bad”: redundant narrative evidence interacted with the
meaning of the action.

## What it means

The clean claim is behavioral and local to one pinned model and game family:
correct narrative evidence can impair assertion-based strategic action without
impairing independent consequence reports. The internal cause remains open. It
could involve retrieval, an assertion script, or a persistent policy state; a
future activation-interchange study must distinguish those possibilities.
